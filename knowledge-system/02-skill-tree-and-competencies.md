# B. Sales Communication Skill Tree & C. Competency Model

## Why this structure
Your proposed tree had 9 parallel categories. The books suggest a **sequence** instead. Voss's **Behavioral Change Stairway** (NSTD Ch.5) puts it as: *active listening → empathy → rapport → influence → behavior change*. You can't climb a step you skipped. Carnegie's Part 2 ("make people like you") comes before Part 3 ("win people to your way of thinking") for the same reason. So the tree is arranged in **tiers**: lower tiers unlock higher ones in the product. Your original categories all still appear, mapped to tiers.

Coverage tags: **●** Strong source support · **◐** Partial · **○** Gap (thin, needs sales-specific sources later). IDs (e.g. `LIS.MIRROR`) are the stable keys used in the rubric, scenarios and database.

## C. Competency Model (5 levels, observable behaviors)

Levels: **L1 Beginner · L2 Developing · L3 Competent · L4 Advanced · L5 Expert.** Each level describes what an observer (or the AI evaluator) can *see in a transcript*. A level is reached when its behaviors show up consistently across ≥3 sessions (see `04-rubrics.md` §Mapping).

### SELF.REGULATE · Emotional self-control
| L | Observable behaviors |
|---|---|
| 1 | Matches hostility with hostility, or caves right away. Long defensive explanations when challenged. |
| 2 | Avoids hostility but over-apologizes. Concedes to end the discomfort. |
| 3 | Stays calm under a single attack. Answers with a question or acknowledgment rather than a defense. |
| 4 | Stays calm under repeated pressure. Pauses deliberately. Holds a position without heat. |
| 5 | Uses pressure moments on purpose (labels the tension, slows the pace). Pressure visibly doesn't change how they negotiate. |

### SELF.NONEEDY · Walk-away mindset
| L | Observable behaviors |
|---|---|
| 1 | Begs ("Please, just give it a try"). Makes discounts unprompted. |
| 2 | Doesn't beg, but rushes to close and fills silences with concessions. |
| 3 | Comfortable hearing "no". Doesn't change price without being asked to. |
| 4 | States limits calmly. Can say "This might not be the right fit" and mean it. |
| 5 | Qualifies the prospect in or out openly. Accepts a no-deal as a good outcome when it is one. |

### SELF.PREP · Preparation
| L | Observable behaviors |
|---|---|
| 1 | No preparation. Wings it. |
| 2 | Knows own offer and price, nothing about the prospect. |
| 3 | Written goal + 3 calibrated questions + 3 accusation-audit items. Uses at least half of them. |
| 4 | Full one sheet including behind-the-table questions and non-cash items. Adapts it live. |
| 5 | Prepares hypotheses and industry insider questions. Debriefs prep against the outcome every time. |

### COM.CLARITY / COM.CONCISE · Clarity & conciseness
| L | Observable behaviors |
|---|---|
| 1 | Rambles, uses jargon, prospect asks "sorry, what do you do?" |
| 2 | Understandable but feature-led and long-winded (>60 words per turn as a habit). |
| 3 | Benefit-led statement of value in ≤25 words. Turns mostly ≤40 words. |
| 4 | Adjusts depth to the counterpart's style (data for Analysts, bottom line for Assertives). Prices and boundaries stated in one or two sentences. |
| 5 | Every statement has a purpose. Uses the prospect's vocabulary. Explains complex offers with an analogy from the prospect's world. |

### COM.TONE · Tone & mood match
| L | Observable behaviors |
|---|---|
| 1 | One fixed register (e.g. salesy enthusiasm) regardless of the prospect's mood. |
| 2 | Notices mood but adapts late or clumsily. |
| 3 | First response matches the prospect's register. Warm/playful default. |
| 4 | Changes tone on purpose: calm and slow for price and boundaries, light for rapport. |
| 5 | Uses tone to shift the prospect's emotional state (e.g. calms an angry customer within 2–3 turns). |

### COM.OPENING · Openings
| L | Observable behaviors |
|---|---|
| 1 | Launches into a pitch. Never checks timing. |
| 2 | Introduces self, asks "Do you have a minute?" (invites a reflexive no). |
| 3 | Brief benefit intro + no-oriented permission ("Did I catch you at a bad time?"). |
| 4 | Adds a relevant, researched reason for calling and a soft accusation audit for cold calls. |
| 5 | Opening fits the specific person (insider hook, referral, trigger event). Prospect asks the first question. |

### LIS.ATTEND · Attending & talk ratio
| L | Observable behaviors |
|---|---|
| 1 | Interrupts. Talks >70% of the time in discovery. Plans the next pitch instead of listening. |
| 2 | Lets the prospect finish but ignores what they said. |
| 3 | Follow-ups reference the previous answer. Prospect talk share ≥50% in discovery. |
| 4 | Picks up offhand remarks and side details and explores them. |
| 5 | Notices what *isn't* said, and links remarks from different points in the conversation. |

### LIS.MIRROR / LIS.PAUSE · Mirroring & silence
| L | Observable behaviors |
|---|---|
| 1 | Never mirrors. Fills every silence. |
| 2 | Mirrors mechanically, then talks straight away. |
| 3 | 2+ mirrors in discovery, each followed by a pause, and the prospect elaborates. |
| 4 | Mirrors at key moments (vague claims, emotional words). Uses silence after labels and prices. |
| 5 | Mirroring is invisible and natural. Silence is used strategically, including in bargaining. |

### LIS.SUMMARY · Summarizing to "that's right"
| L | Observable behaviors |
|---|---|
| 1 | Never summarizes. |
| 2 | Summarizes facts only ("So you need a website"). |
| 3 | Summary = paraphrase + label. Earns agreement. |
| 4 | Earns a genuine "that's right" before making any recommendation. Re-summarizes when given "you're right". |
| 5 | Summarizes the prospect's world better than they could, including the stakes for people behind the table. |

### EMP.LABEL · Labeling
| L | Observable behaviors |
|---|---|
| 1 | Ignores emotion, or says "I understand" as filler. |
| 2 | Labels with "I hear that…" or generic statements. |
| 3 | Uses "It seems/sounds like…" specific to the prospect's content, at emotional moments. |
| 4 | Labels fears and barriers before they're voiced. Labels positive dynamics to reinforce them. |
| 5 | Labels are accurate enough that the prospect visibly relaxes and opens up. Uses labels to reveal hidden information. |

### EMP.AUDIT · Accusation audit
| L | Observable behaviors |
|---|---|
| 1 | Hides bad news or price until the end. Gets defensive when accused. |
| 2 | Responds to accusations after they're made. |
| 3 | Says 1–2 likely negative perceptions before the ask or bad news. |
| 4 | Full audit (3+ items) delivered calmly with pauses. Prospect argues the opposite. |
| 5 | Audit fits the specific person. Uses it to reset tense calls in the middle. |

### RAP.INTEREST / RAP.APPRECIATE · Interest & appreciation
| L | Observable behaviors |
|---|---|
| 1 | Self-focused talk. Generic flattery. |
| 2 | Polite small talk unconnected to the business. |
| 3 | Follows up on 2+ personal or business details. Gives specific compliments when deserved. |
| 4 | Remembers and reuses details later in the call. Gives appreciation that ties to the prospect's pride points. |
| 5 | The prospect volunteers things they hadn't planned to share. |

### RAP.STYLE · Adapting to counterpart style
| L | Observable behaviors |
|---|---|
| 1 | Same approach for everyone. |
| 2 | Notices style differences but doesn't adapt. |
| 3 | Adapts pace (slows with Analysts, gets to the point with Assertives). |
| 4 | Adapts tactics: data and time for Analysts, probing for hidden objections with Accommodators, listening and summarizing first with Assertives. |
| 5 | Identifies style within 2–3 turns and switches smoothly when a second stakeholder joins. |

### DIS.CALIBRATED · Questioning
| L | Observable behaviors |
|---|---|
| 1 | Almost no questions, or only "Are you interested?" |
| 2 | Mostly closed or leading questions. Uses "why" accusingly. |
| 3 | ≥60% open What/How questions. Follow-ups built on answers. |
| 4 | Question sequences that move from situation → pain → cost → decision. Asks 2–3 related questions from different angles. |
| 5 | Questions make the prospect reflect ("Good question, I hadn't thought about that"). Uncovers motives and hidden concerns. |

### DIS.PAIN / DIS.GOALS · Pains, goals, cost of inaction
| L | Observable behaviors |
|---|---|
| 1 | Assumes the pain. Pitches straight away. |
| 2 | Identifies a surface pain ("site is old"). |
| 3 | Uncovers the business impact ("losing online orders"). |
| 4 | Gets the prospect to quantify the cost of inaction in their own numbers. Links pain to personal stakes. |
| 5 | Uncovers the emotional and personal driver (pride, fear, status) behind the business goal. |

### DIS.DECISION / DIS.QUALIFY · Decision process & qualification ○
| L | Observable behaviors |
|---|---|
| 1 | Never asks who else is involved. |
| 2 | Asks "Are you the decision maker?" (closed and can cause friction). |
| 3 | Asks "How does a decision like this get made on your side?" |
| 4 | Identifies people behind the table and their concerns ("How on board is your partner?"). |
| 5 | Plans how to equip the internal champion. Spots authority signals in pronoun use. |

### DIS.BLACKSWAN · Hidden information
| L | Observable behaviors |
|---|---|
| 1 | Takes everything at face value. |
| 2 | Asks one "anything else?" question. |
| 3 | Probes constraints and timing drivers. Sometimes uncovers hidden info. |
| 4 | Treats apparent irrationality as a clue. Regularly uncovers the scenario's Black Swan. |
| 5 | Uncovers it early and changes the whole approach around it. |

### INF.VALUE / INF.FRAME · Value & framing
| L | Observable behaviors |
|---|---|
| 1 | Feature dump about self/company. |
| 2 | Generic benefits ("save time and money"). |
| 3 | Benefits linked to the prospect's stated goals. |
| 4 | Frames value against the prospect's quantified cost of inaction. Uses loss framing honestly. |
| 5 | Prospect states the value themselves ("So basically this pays for itself if…"). |

### INF.OWNERSHIP / INF.STORY · Ownership & storytelling
| L | Observable behaviors |
|---|---|
| 1 | Tells the prospect what they need. |
| 2 | Recommends after some questions but in their own terms. |
| 3 | Builds the recommendation from the prospect's words. Uses one concrete example. |
| 4 | Offers 2 options and lets the prospect choose and refine. Relevant short story with an outcome. |
| 5 | Prospect proposes the solution the seller intended. Stories are tailored and vivid without being long. |

### OBJ.* · Objection handling
| L | Observable behaviors |
|---|---|
| 1 | Argues, contradicts ("That's not true"), or drops it right away. |
| 2 | Responds with a rehearsed rebuttal without understanding the objection. |
| 3 | Acknowledges/labels first, then asks a calibrated question to understand. |
| 4 | Separates the stated objection from the real one. Addresses the real one. Uses an audit to head off predictable objections. |
| 5 | Objections become discovery opportunities. Prospect ends up answering their own objection. |

### NEG.ANCHOR / NEG.CONCEDE / NEG.REFUSE · Negotiation
| L | Observable behaviors |
|---|---|
| 1 | Names a low number first. Splits the difference. Concedes without asking anything back. |
| 2 | Holds price once, then caves under a second push. |
| 3 | Gets their expectation first or anchors with a range. Asks for something in return for every concession. |
| 4 | Shrinking concessions. Refuses gracefully ("How am I supposed to do that?"). Moves to non-monetary terms. |
| 5 | Shapes how the prospect perceives fairness and value before numbers come up. Closes near target with the relationship intact. |

### CLO.* · Commitment & closing
| L | Observable behaviors |
|---|---|
| 1 | Never asks. The call ends with "Let me know!" |
| 2 | Asks once with a closed yes/no question and accepts a vague answer. |
| 3 | Clear ask + concrete next step (owner, date, action). |
| 4 | No-oriented trial close. Rule of Three confirmation. Checks for people behind the table. |
| 5 | Commitment is co-designed and implementation risks are dealt with before the call ends. Follow-up is pre-agreed. |

### DIF.ANGER / DIF.BADNEWS / DIF.BOUNDARY · Difficult conversations
| L | Observable behaviors |
|---|---|
| 1 | Defends, blames, or caves. |
| 2 | Apologizes generically. Explains too early. |
| 3 | Lets the person vent, labels, owns their share of the fault, and only then problem-solves. |
| 4 | Delivers bad news with an audit first. Keeps a boundary consistent across repeated pushes. |
| 5 | Turns conflict into stronger trust. The other person leaves feeling respected even when the answer is no. |

### DIF.FEEDBACK · Giving feedback (Carnegie Part 4)
| L | Observable behaviors |
|---|---|
| 1 | Blunt criticism, often in front of others. |
| 2 | Praise-then-"but" sandwich. |
| 3 | Talks about own similar mistakes. Asks questions rather than giving orders. |
| 4 | Points out the issue indirectly, makes it look easy to fix, and lets the person save face. |
| 5 | Gives the person a reputation to live up to. Follows up and praises improvement. |
